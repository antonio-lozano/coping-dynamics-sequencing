Early-life stress · Behavioural sequencing

Two ways of coping with the same early-life stress

Mice raised under limited bedding and nesting behave differently when a second threat arrives in adulthood. Sequencing their movement rather than scoring it splits them in two: a vulnerable group whose behaviour turns repetitive and stereotyped, and a resilient group that, on every measure separating the vulnerable animals from controls, is indistinguishable from a control.

The repertoire

Stop naming the stress response. Measure it.

Cannon called it fight or flight in 1925. Bracha added freeze and fawn in 2004. The list has kept growing ever since — fold, flop, feign, fret, falter — one label for every new observation, and no measurement underneath any of them.

So we stopped naming and started sequencing. Keypoint-MoSeq segments pose tracking into short recurring motifs without supervision. The motifs group into seven behaviours a human can recognise, and every session becomes a sequence of them at 250 ms resolution — which means coping can be described by what an animal does next, not only by how much of each thing it does.

  • Sniffing
  • Grooming

The paradigm

Stress early, then a second threat

Adversity in the first days of life, then a fear challenge in adulthood. The question is not whether the stressed animals freeze more or less, but whether their behaviour is organised differently when the threat returns.

P2 – P9

Early-life stress

Limited bedding and nesting, the chronic-adversity model of Rice et al., 2008.

P60

Fear conditioning

A 30 s tone at 2.8 kHz and 80 dB, paired with a 2 s foot shock at 0.4 mA.

Two profiles

The average hides the effect. The split reveals it.

Pooled across all stressed animals, the sequence measures move in the right direction but stop at trends. Separate the two coping profiles and the picture resolves: on every measure that survives FDR correction between vulnerable animals and controls, resilient animals do not differ from controls at all.

Reproduce

Three commands, identical bytes

Every figure, table, statistic and report on this page rebuilds from the data in the repository. There are no hidden inputs and no download step: a clone is the whole package.

# install the locked environment
uv sync --locked

# verify the shipped artifacts against MANIFEST.csv
uv run python scripts/check_reproducibility.py

# rebuild everything — the tree ends clean
uv run python scripts/run_all.py

The repository referees itself

MANIFEST.csv carries a SHA-256 for every artifact. A rebuild that changes one byte fails the check, on Windows and on Linux alike.

Statistics fitted once

Each model is fitted in one place and exported; scripts/check_manuscript.py pins every statistic quoted in the manuscript to the shipped report.

A companion classifier

tools/behavior_dlc_classifier/ ships the trained DeepLabCut networks, demo videos, tests and a guide, so new recordings can be tracked and classified without training anything.
